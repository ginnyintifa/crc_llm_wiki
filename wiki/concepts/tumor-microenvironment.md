# Tumor Microenvironment (TME)

## Definition
The tumor microenvironment is the non-cancer-cell component of a tumor — the ecosystem of recruited and reprogrammed normal cells, extracellular matrix, vasculature, signaling molecules, and physical/chemical gradients that collectively support or restrain tumor growth. Hanahan and Coussens (2012) coined "accessories to the crime" to describe how TME cells contribute to hallmark capabilities. The TME is not just a bystander — it is an active driver of tumorigenesis, metastasis, and therapy resistance.

## Key Cell Types in the CRC TME

### [[CAFs]] (Cancer-Associated Fibroblasts)
- Derived from: resident colon fibroblasts, mesenchymal stem cells, endothelial cells (EndMT)
- Main subtypes: **myoCAFs** (contractile, ECM-producing) and **iCAFs** (inflammatory, cytokine-secreting)
- Functions: ECM remodeling (desmoplastic stroma in CRC), paracrine growth factors (HGF, EGF), metabolic symbiosis, immune suppression
- CRC relevance: desmoplastic reaction in CRC is CAF-driven; dense stroma is a barrier to drug delivery and T cell infiltration

### [[TAMs]] (Tumor-Associated Macrophages)
- Spectrum from M1-like (tumor-antagonizing) to M2-like (tumor-promoting)
- M2-like TAMs in CRC: promote angiogenesis, suppress T cells, facilitate invasion
- Source: bone marrow-derived monocytes + tissue-resident Kupffer cells (in liver mets)
- In CRC liver metastasis: TAMs in the hepatic metastatic niche are key supporters of outgrowth

### Tumor-Associated Neutrophils (TANs)
- Analogous spectrum to TAMs: N1 (tumor-antagonizing) to N2 (tumor-promoting)
- N2 TANs: suppress T cells, promote angiogenesis, assist intravasation
- MDSCs: immature myeloid cells with immunosuppressive function

### T cells
- CD8+ cytotoxic T cells: the primary anti-tumor effectors; subject to exhaustion in CRC TME
- CD4+ helper T cells: support CD8+ and B cell responses
- Tregs (FOXP3+): suppress CD8+ and CD4+ effectors; enriched in MSS CRC TME
- CRC: MSI-H tumors have high TIL infiltration and respond to ICIs; MSS tumors have immunosuppressive TME and are ICI-refractory

### Endothelial cells and pericytes
- Angiogenic neovasculature: leaky, chaotic, pericyte-poor → enables intravasation
- Immunosuppressive: impair T cell extravasation, express PD-L1 and other checkpoint ligands
- Vascular co-option (in liver mets): CRC liver metastases use this more than angiogenesis
- HEVs and TLS: associated with better prognosis in CRC

### Neurons / innervation
- Emerging role in CRC: perineural invasion is a recognized pathological feature
- Sympathetic/parasympathetic innervation may promote invasion and immune evasion
- CRC-specific innervation biology largely unexplored (open area)

### Senescent cells
- Tumor-promoting SASP variants of fibroblasts, endothelial cells, TAMs in CRC TME
- SASPs can stimulate proliferative signaling in adjacent cancer cells

## Physical TME
- **Matrix stiffness**: increased in CRC stroma; activates mechanotransduction (YAP/TAZ) driving invasion
- **Hypoxia**: drives HIF-1α → VEGF → angiogenesis; also promotes EMP and immune evasion
- **Acidosis**: extracellular acidosis in CRC TME impairs T cell function
- **Interstitial pressure**: elevated in CRC tumors; impedes drug delivery

## CRC TME vs. Other Cancers
- CRC TME is generally less immune-infiltrated than melanoma or lung cancer (in MSS)
- MSI-H CRC is a notable exception — "hot" tumor with high neoantigen load and TIL
- CRC desmoplastic stroma is particularly prominent → barrier to immunotherapy
- Gut microbiome directly shapes the CRC TME through microbial metabolites and immune cell programming
