# Hanahan (2026) — Hallmarks of Cancer: Then and Now, and Beyond

## Citation
Hanahan, D. (2026). Hallmarks of cancer—Then and now, and beyond. *Cell 189*, April 16, 2026.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.12.049
*(Raw file: `raw/hanahan-2026-hallmarks-cancer.pdf`)*

## Summary
This review by Douglas Hanahan synthesizes 26 years of the hallmarks of cancer conceptualization across six publications (2000–2026). It consolidates the framework into four conceptual dimensions: (1) nine acquired functional capabilities (the hallmarks), (2) five hallmark-enabling phenotypic characteristics, (3) seven classes of hallmark-conveying cells in the tumor microenvironment, and (4) systemic interactions (aging, obesity, etc.). A corollary hypothesis proposes that "hallmark co-targeting" — simultaneous disruption of multiple independent hallmark mechanisms — could overcome adaptive resistance to monotherapies.

## Key Findings

### The 9 Hallmarks (acquired functional capabilities)
1. Sustaining proliferative signaling — KRAS (~30% all tumors, ~50% CRC) is the canonical driver
2. Inactivating growth suppressors — APC (WNT gatekeeper), TP53, RB, CDKNs
3. Resisting programmed cell death — BCL-2/BCL-XL/MCL-1 upregulation; also ferroptosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis resistance
4. Establishing replicative immortality — TERT reactivation; ALT in mesenchymal/neuroepithelial tumors
5. Inducing or accessing vasculature — angiogenesis (VEGFA, ANGPT2) + vascular co-option
6. Deregulating cellular metabolism — metabolic hybrid states; lactate, glutamine; TME-dictated
7. **Unlocking phenotypic plasticity** — intra-lineage (dedifferentiation, CSCs) + trans-lineage (EMP); TP53 loss enables plasticity
8. Evading immune destruction — T cell exhaustion; immunosuppressive TME; virtually every TME cell type contributes
9. Activating invasion and metastasis — no universal "metastasis genes"; CIN, EMP, metabolic plasticity, premetastatic niche are key

### The 5 Enabling Phenotypic Characteristics
1. Loss of genomic integrity (CIN, point mutations, ecDNA, chromothripsis)
2. Non-mutational epigenetic reprogramming (DNA methylation, histone code, TF availability)
3. Tumor-promoting inflammation (TAMs, TANs, wound-healing co-option; KRAS/TP53 recruit myeloid cells)
4. **Innervation** (sympathetic, parasympathetic, somatosensory axons; synaptic/pseudo-synaptic connections to cancer cells)
5. **Polymorphic microbiomes** (gut microbiome as prototype; modulates immune evasion and inflammation)

### The 7 TME Cell Classes
1. Cancer cells (including CSC spectrum — "cancer stemness" continuum)
2. Endothelial cells and pericytes (angiogenic vs. co-opted; immunosuppressive; HEVs, TLS)
3. [[CAFs]] — myoCAFs, iCAFs; ECM remodeling; metabolic symbiosis; immune suppression
4. [[TAMs]] and tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) — M1/M2 spectrum; MDSCs; stimulate proliferation, angiogenesis, metastasis
5. Additional immune cells — Tregs (FOXP3), Bregs, dysfunctional DCs and NK cells
6. Neurons and axonal projections — innervation stimulates proliferation, immune evasion, invasion
7. Senescent cells — SASP can be tumor-promoting (senescent CAFs, TAMs, tumor endothelial cells)

### Hallmark Co-targeting (therapeutic rationale)
- **Proven:** VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors + ICIs (renal, lung, hepatocellular, endometrial)
- **Proven:** bevacizumab + olaparib (PARP) in ovarian cancer with impaired DNA repair
- **Future:** ICIs + TME immunosuppressive cell reprogramming (CAFs, TAMs, TANs)
- Logic: simultaneous adaptive resistance to multiple mechanistically independent targets is harder to achieve

## CRC-Specific Points
- KRAS mutated in ~50% CRC (explicitly cited); associated with all 9 hallmarks AND tumor-promoting inflammation
- APC named as WNT gatekeeper encoding the key growth suppressor β-catenin degrader
- TP53 loss: enables dedifferentiation and lineage plasticity (relevant to CRC progression)
- Gut microbiome (polymorphic microbiome): highly relevant to CRC; affects immune evasion and inflammation
- EMP (formerly EMT) as hybrid states: relevant to CRC CTCs, liver/peritoneal colonization
- Obesity connection: epidemiological link to CRC; leptin/adiponectin imbalance promotes hallmarks

## Methods
Review article. No primary experimental data. Synthesizes literature from 1000+ references. Figures 1–5 illustrate the 4-dimensional framework and co-targeting rationale.

## Gaps / Open Questions Raised
- No clinically approved drugs for: telomerase/ALT inhibition, metabolic diversity disruption, invasion/metastasis inhibition, phenotypic plasticity shutdown
- Innervation role in CRC specifically: not elaborated in this review, open area
- Microbiome specific species relevant to CRC metastasis: mentioned generally, not detailed
- Mechanisms by which SASP dichotomously promotes vs. suppresses tumors remain unclear
- Additional hallmark parameters may exist — paper explicitly leaves this open
