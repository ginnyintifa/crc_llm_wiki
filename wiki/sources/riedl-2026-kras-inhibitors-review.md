# Riedl 2026 — Emerging landscape of KRAS inhibitors in cancer treatment

## Citation
Riedl JM, Matsubara H, McNeil R, Patel PS, Fece de la Cruz F, Gulhan DC & Corcoran RB. "Emerging landscape of KRAS inhibitors in cancer treatment." *Cancer Cell* 44, 471–490 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2026.01.001. Corresponding author: Ryan B. Corcoran (MGH Cancer Center / Harvard Medical School).

## Summary
This comprehensive review synthesizes the clinical and preclinical landscape of KRAS-targeted therapies as of early 2026. Starting from the biology of oncogenic RAS mutations and the structural breakthrough enabling mutant-selective KRAS^G12C OFF-state inhibitors (sotorasib, adagrasib), the review delineates the molecular mechanisms of primary and acquired resistance, then surveys three emerging classes of novel (K)RAS inhibitors: (1) mutant-selective inhibitors (G12C and G12D), (2) isoform-selective "pan-KRAS" inhibitors, and (3) pan-RAS inhibitors. CRC receives particular attention as a tumor type with distinctive adaptive resistance through EGFR-mediated RAS reactivation, motivating the KRAS inhibitor + anti-EGFR combinations now approved or in late-stage trials. The review positions YAP/TAZ/TEAD signaling and EMT as key non-genetic resistance axes and identifies combined targeting strategies as priority future directions.

## Key Findings

### 1. RAS mutation landscape across tumor types
- RAS mutations in ~20% of all cancers (~3.4 million new cases/year globally)
- In CRC: KRAS G12D ~12.6%, G12V ~8.8%, G12C ~2.9%, G13D ~7.2%, Q61X ~2.1% (Fig. 1B)
- KRAS^G12C accounts for only ~3% of all KRAS mutations — limiting the patient coverage of approved G12C inhibitors
- KRAS^G12D is most prevalent in CRC and PDAC; KRAS^G12C most prevalent in NSCLC
- Biochemical relevance: G12C retains partial GTP hydrolysis → accessible GDP-bound state → covalent OFF-state targeting feasible; G12D predominantly GTP-bound (altered electrostatics) → requires ON-state or non-covalent approaches; G12V has near-zero intrinsic hydrolysis → always active

### 2. First-generation KRAS^G12C OFF-state inhibitors: clinical evidence

**Single-agent activity:**
| Drug | Tumor | ORR (%) | mPFS (mo) | Key trial |
|------|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Sotorasib | NSCLC | 37.1 | 6.8 | Codebreak 100 Ph2 |
| Sotorasib | CRC | 9.7 | 4.0 | Codebreak 100 Ph2 |
| Adagrasib | NSCLC | 42.9 | 6.5 | KRYSTAL-1 Ph2 |
| Adagrasib | CRC | 23.3 | 5.6 | KRYSTAL-1 Ph2 |

**CRC combination (anti-EGFR):**
| Combination | ORR (%) | mPFS (mo) | mOS (mo) | Trial |
|------------|---------|-----------|----------|-------|
| Sotorasib + panitumumab | 30.2 | 5.6 | — | Codebreak 300 (Ph3; FDA approved) |
| Adagrasib + cetuximab | 34.0 | 6.9 | 15.9 | KRYSTAL-1 Ph1/2 |
| Divarasib + cetuximab | 62.5 | 8.5 | — | Ph1b |
| Olomorasib + cetuximab | 43.0 | 7.4 | — | LOXO-RAS-20001 |

Key clinical observation: **KRAS^G12C ORR in CRC is roughly half that of NSCLC** for all agents. Adding anti-EGFR approximately doubles ORR in CRC, largely equaling NSCLC single-agent responses. This is mechanistically explained by EGFR being the dominant driver of adaptive RAS/MAPK pathway reactivation in CRC.

### 3. Mechanisms of resistance to KRAS^G12C inhibitors

**Primary resistance (10–30% of patients)**
- Preexisting secondary RAS co-mutations (rare in treatment-naïve)
- **KEAP1 loss-of-function** mutations: strongest validated predictor of poor response; emerge consistently across NSCLC data; may reflect broader aggressive tumor biology
- TP53 and PIK3CA co-mutations: associated with resistance in CRC/PDAC preclinically; clinical relevance uncertain
- SMARCA4, CDKN2A co-mutations in NSCLC

**Acquired genetic resistance (~60% of patients at progression)**
- On-target KRAS alterations:
  - KRAS amplification (increases target protein beyond inhibitory capacity)
  - Secondary activating KRAS mutations at G12D, G13D, or Q61H (arise in *trans* on wild-type allele — not targetable by mutant-selective G12C inhibitors)
  - Switch II pocket mutations (R68, M72, H95, Y96) — prevent drug binding
- Off-target alterations (most common):
  - **RTK amplification/mutation/fusion**: EGFR, ERBB2, FGFR, c-MET (especially EGFR in CRC)
  - BRAF mutations; MAP2K1 mutations
  - NRAS/HRAS amplifications or activating mutations (bypass via WT RAS isoforms)
  - PIK3CA mutations; MYC amplification; TP53 loss (less well defined functionally)

**Non-genetic resistance (~25% of patients)**
- **RTK-mediated adaptive MAPK reactivation**: relief of negative feedback → upregulation of RTKs (EGFR, HER2, FGFR, c-MET) → WT RAS activation (bypassing mutant KRAS inhibition) AND shift of mutant KRAS toward GTP-bound state; particularly prominent in CRC
- **YAP/TAZ/TEAD signaling**: KRAS inhibition → disrupts membrane localization of Scribble → SHOC2/PP1c complex forms → YAP nuclear translocation → TEAD-mediated transcription (including MRAS, RAS superfamily protein); YAP + pan-TEAD inhibitor combination is synergistic in preclinical models
- **EMT**: epithelial cells lose apical-basal polarity and cell-cell adhesion, acquire mesenchymal motility/invasiveness; driven by ZEB1, SNAI1/2, TWIST; increasingly recognized as a key resistance mechanism
- **Histologic transformation**: adenocarcinoma → squamous cell carcinoma (NSCLC); alveolar/mucinous differentiation shifts; reflects transcriptional reprogramming reducing KRAS dependency

### 4. Three classes of novel (K)RAS inhibitors

**Class 1: Mutant-selective inhibitors**
Cover one specific KRAS mutant; spare other KRAS mutants, WT-KRAS, NRAS, HRAS. Best tolerability; narrowest coverage.

*Next-gen G12C inhibitors (selected):*
- **Divarasib**: 5–20× more potent than sotorasib/adagrasib; NSCLC ORR 55.6%, mPFS 13.8 months (Ph1); CRC Ph1b ongoing
- **Glecirasib**: NSCLC ORR 47.9% (Ph2); CRC 22.7% single-agent, 50% + cetuximab
- **IBI351**: NSCLC ORR 49.1% (Ph2); CRC 47.5% (ORR, Ph1/2)
- **Garsorasib**, **D3S-001**, **Olomorasib**, **Opnurasib**, **Sosimerasib**: all in early-phase trials with promising signals

*G12C ON-state inhibitors (tri-complex):*
- **Elironrasib (RMC-6291)**: molecular glue with cyclophilin A (CYPA); covalent ON-state; NSCLC G12C-naive ORR 43–50%, G12C-pretreated ORR 50%; FDA breakthrough therapy; overcomes switch II pocket resistance mutations
- **BBO-8520, FMC-376**: ON/OFF dual-state covalent G12C inhibitors; active against G12C-amplified and RTK-overexpressing resistant tumors

*G12D-selective inhibitors:*
- **MRTX1133** (Mirati/now terminated Ph1): first potent non-covalent G12D OFF-state switch II pocket inhibitor; preclinical proof-of-concept; Phase 1 clinical trial terminated
- **Zoldonrasib (RMC-9805)**: CYPA tricomplex, ON-state covalent G12D; NSCLC ORR 61%, PDAC ORR 30% (Ph1/2)
- **GFH375 (VS-7375)**: ON/OFF-state noncovalent G12D; NSCLC ORR 58%, PDAC ORR 41%, mPFS 5.5 months (Ph1/2)
- **ASP3082**: first KRAS^G12D PROTAC (targeted protein degradation); ORR 38% in Ph1
- HRS-4642, INCB161734, LY3962673, QTX3046, AZD0022, TSN1611: various G12D Ph1 trials

**Class 2: Isoform-selective "pan-KRAS" inhibitors**
Cover multiple KRAS mutants + wild-type KRAS; spare NRAS, HRAS. Cover >90% of KRAS-mutant patients. Moderate tolerability.

- **BI-2865 / BI-3706674**: non-covalent, targets GDP-bound OFF state; broad in vitro + in vivo activity across G12C, G12D, G12V, A146V, and KRAS-amplified models; clinical testing ongoing
- Others: LY4066434, ALTA-3263, PF-07985045/07934040, QTX3034, QTX3544, ERAS-4001, BBO-11818, AMG410

**Class 3: Pan-RAS inhibitors**
Target all RAS isoforms (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS), mutant and wild-type; broadest coverage; highest theoretical toxicity concern.

- **Daraxonrasib (RMC-6236)**: CYPA tricomplex ON-state, non-covalent; Phase 3 for NSCLC + PDAC; NSCLC KRAS^G12X ORR 38%, mPFS 9.8 months, mOS 17.7 months; PDAC KRAS^G12X ORR 29%; FDA breakthrough therapy; overcomes acquired resistance to G12C inhibitors; manageable toxicity (well tolerated at therapeutic doses due to preferential engagement of mutant GTP-bound form)
- **ERAS-0015**: pan-RAS molecular glue, Phase 1

### 5. Combination and future strategies
- **Mutant-selective + pan-RAS**: combining daraxonrasib with elironrasib (G12C) or zoldonrasib (G12D) in Phase 1 trials — rationale is potent ON-state suppression from pan-RAS + sustained OFF-state suppression from mutant-selective
- **KRAS + anti-EGFR in CRC**: now validated; sotorasib + panitumumab FDA approved; multiple combinations in Phase 3 trials
- **KRAS + ICI**: adagrasib + pembrolizumab (KRYSTAL-7 Ph2 in NSCLC): ORR 61%, mPFS 27.7 months in PD-L1 >50% subgroup; promising
- **KRAS + YAP/TEAD inhibition**: synergistic in preclinical (KRAS inhibition → YAP nuclear translocation → TEAD activation → resistance); combination blocks both MAPK reactivation and YAP bypass
- **Vertical pathway inhibition**: KRAS + SOS1, SHP2, RAF, MEK, ERK co-targeting
- **PROTAC approach (ASP3082)**: targeted degradation; active in KRAS-amplified models

## Contradictions / Tensions with Existing Wiki Content

> ⚠️ **YAP tension partially resolved**: This review provides the molecular mechanism by which KRAS inhibition paradoxically activates YAP: KRAS^G12C inhibition → disrupts Scribble membrane localization → SHOC2/PP1c complex forms → YAP nuclear translocation → TEAD-mediated transcription. This explains how YAP activity is elevated after KRAS inhibition (consistent with Heinlein 2026 showing YAP program in MAPK-high cells) while not necessarily being the direct driver of EMP1 expression (consistent with Cañellas-Socias 2022). YAP is a resistance bypass pathway, not the initiating driver of HRC identity.

> ⚠️ **MRTX1133 Phase 1 terminated**: The KRAS entity page (from Heinlein 2026) cites MRTX1133 as a preclinical tool compound and emerging therapeutic. This review notes the Phase 1 clinical trial (NCT05737706) was terminated. Preclinical validation (Heinlein 2026) remains valid but the clinical development path has shifted to other G12D agents (zoldonrasib, GFH375, ASP3082).

> ⚠️ **Adaptive WNT reactivation (complements Heinlein 2026)**: Heinlein 2026 showed WNT/LGR5 reactivation as a consequence of KRAS^G12D inhibition (MRTX1133). This review frames the same phenomenon in a broader resistance context: RTK-mediated RAS/MAPK reactivation is the primary adaptive mechanism, while the WNT shift may represent a secondary cell-state adaptation. These are complementary views.

## Gaps / Open Questions Raised
- Can G12D inhibitors (zoldonrasib, GFH375) achieve durable responses in CRC where EGFR adaptive reactivation is dominant? Anti-EGFR combination trials are needed.
- Is KEAP1 mutation also a strong predictor of KRAS^G12D inhibitor resistance in CRC?
- Can YAP/TEAD co-inhibition prevent adaptive resistance in the clinical setting?
- Do pan-RAS inhibitors provide durable responses despite broader toxicity concerns?
- What is the optimal sequencing of KRAS inhibitors and immunotherapy in CRC (unlike NSCLC, CRC is predominantly MSS)?
